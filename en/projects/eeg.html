Epileptic seizure detection from EEG signals

One Random Forest model per patient, on the public CHB-MIT database. Signal filtering, time, frequency and dynamic features, channel selection with Boruta, and a decision threshold tuned so that seizures are not missed.

The problem

Automatically spot epileptic seizures in 23-channel EEG recordings. Missing a seizure costs more than a false alarm, so recall matters more than precision alone.

My approach

  1. Filtering50 Hz notch, 0.5 to 30 Hz band-pass, common average reference
  2. Windows4 s with a 1 s step, labelled seizure if over 60% overlaps a seizure
  3. FeaturesTime, frequency, wavelets and non-linear dynamics
  4. ChannelsBoruta keeps the useful channels for each patient
  5. ModelRandom Forest per patient, threshold tuned for recall
  • Time features: variance, kurtosis, interquartile range, line length, Hjorth mobility.
  • Frequency features: band power, discrete (db4) and continuous (Morlet) wavelets.
  • Signal dynamics: Shannon and permutation entropy, Hurst exponent, correlation dimension.
  • One model per patient, because what a seizure looks like varies a lot from one person to another.

Limits

One model per patient, evaluated on files that contain seizures: the results do not carry over as-is to a continuous recording or to a new patient.

The test set holds few seizures per patient (1 to 4): recall therefore rests on a small number of events.

False detections vary across patients: up to 9.5% for patient 13.
